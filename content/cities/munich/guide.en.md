## Visa and taxation

- As an EU citizen, a Spaniard can enter and reside in Germany without a visa, protected by community free movement.
- Tax residents (more than 183 days/year) are taxed on worldwide income in the German personal income tax (Einkommensteuer), with progressive rates of up to 45%.

## Health and medical insurance

- In Germany, health insurance is mandatory for all residents, whether through the public (GKV) or private (PKV) system.
- A Spanish citizen who moves for a short time can use the European Health Card (EHIC), which covers necessary medical care during the first 90 days, but as soon as he establishes himself as a resident (Anmeldung) he must register in the German system.
- For employed workers, the quota is 14.6% of the gross salary distributed between the company and the worker, while self-employed workers and freelancers negotiate their quota directly with the public fund (TK, AOK, Barmer) or can opt for private insurance if they exceed a certain income threshold.

## Neighborhoods and coworkings

- young atmosphere, many cafes with wifi and good moderate nightlife.
- medium-high rents.

## Internet and mobile data

- Activate an eSIM before arriving in Munich and have data from minute one, without depending on the airport Wi-Fi or looking for a physical SIM.
- It is the most practical option for most nomads: it is activated from the mobile phone before even leaving home, without queues or paperwork upon arrival.

## Data sources

- Numbeo Cost of Living Index (without rent), calculated using New York as a reference and converted to euros at the current exchange rate.
- Average fixed broadband speed by country, Speedtest Global Index (Ookla).
- Numbeo Safety Index (residents' perception of safety), rescaled to a score out of 10.
- Numbeo Quality of Life Index (combines purchasing power, health, climate, cost, traffic and pollution), rescaled to a score out of 10.
