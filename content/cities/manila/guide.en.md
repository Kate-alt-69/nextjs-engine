## Visa and taxation

- Spanish citizens can enter the Philippines without a visa and stay for 30 days, extendable at the Bureau of Immigration up to a cumulative maximum of approximately 36 months through successive extensions, with fees of between PHP 6,000-9,000 in the first extension.
- Since 2025, the Philippines has an official digital nomad visa created by Executive Order (EO) 86 of 2025, aimed at remote workers with foreign income of at least USD 24,000/year (~USD 2,000/month).
- Spain will continue to require declaration of worldwide income as long as the nomad maintains his Spanish tax residence.

## Health and medical insurance

- The Philippines has a dual healthcare system: PhilHealth, the mandatory public insurance for local workers, covers only accredited centers within the country through fixed pathology rates well below the real cost in high-end private hospitals — the difference is borne by the patient (so-called 'balance billing').
- Since PhilHealth does not cover non-resident foreigners or medical evacuations, it is essential to purchase international health/travel insurance before settling in Manila — a medical evacuation without coverage can cost between USD 15,000 and USD 50,000.
- For digital nomads and expatriates, the usual route is private healthcare, of very good quality in Metro Manila: hospitals such as Makati Medical Center, St.

## Neighborhoods and coworkings

- Makati is the financial district and the most popular among nomads: very walkable, with numerous coworkings, cafes and shopping centers, and perceived as one of the safest areas of Manila.
- Bonifacio Global City (BGC), in Taguig, is the most modern and international area: wide streets, new buildings, good restaurant life and clean and orderly environment — probably the safest and most comfortable option, although also the most expensive.
- Poblacion (within Makati) is the creative and nightlife neighborhood, with independent cafes and art spaces, although noisier and less quiet to work.

## Internet and mobile data

- Activate an eSIM before arriving in Manila and have data from minute one, without depending on the airport Wi-Fi or looking for a physical SIM.
- It is the most practical option for most nomads: it is activated from the mobile phone before even leaving home, without queues or paperwork upon arrival.
