## Visa and taxation

- The corresponding procedure is the Designated Activities Visa for Digital Nomads, managed by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan (MOFA) through its embassies and consulates.
- As the digital nomad visa only allows 6 months, this threshold is normally not exceeded, so Japanese tax is not paid on income obtained abroad.
- The most common mistake is to believe that the visa automatically exempts you from paying taxes in Japan: in reality, everything depends on not exceeding one year of accumulated stay.

## Neighborhoods and coworkings

- Shibuya is a modern and young neighborhood, with studios for between 120,000 and 160,000 yen per month and one-bedroom apartments (1LDK) for between 210,000 and 300,000 yen.
- Ebisu has an elegant character and a strong presence of expatriates, with prices in the medium-high range.
- Nakameguro, with a creative atmosphere, offers studios from 110,000 to 150,000 yen and 1LDK from 180,000 to 260,000 yen.
- For those looking for coworking, the Kiyosumi-Shirakawa and Jiyugaoka neighborhoods are especially recommended.

## Internet and mobile data

- Activate an eSIM before arriving in Tokyo and have data from minute one, without depending on the airport Wi-Fi or looking for a physical SIM.
- It is the most practical option for most nomads: it is activated from the mobile phone before even leaving home, without queues or paperwork upon arrival.
