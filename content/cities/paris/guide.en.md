## Visa and taxation

- As an EU citizen, a Spaniard can enter, reside and work in France without a visa or residence permit, protected by the free movement of people.
- Fiscally, a tax resident in France (more than 183 days/year) pays French personal income tax, with progressive rates up to 45%, and there is a Spain-France double taxation agreement.

## Health and medical insurance

- France has a universal public health system managed by Assurance Maladie, under the framework of the Protection Universelle Maladie (PUMA).
- A resident Spanish citizen must register with the Caisse Primaire d'Assurance Maladie (CPAM) in their area, providing a passport/DNI and proof of stable residence, to obtain their social security number and, subsequently, the Carte Vitale - the health card that allows automatic reimbursement of medical expenses.
- PUMA guarantees health coverage to any person residing in France on a stable (more than 3 months) and regular basis, covering consultations, hospitalization and medications with a reimbursement percentage (normally 70% in consultations, 80% in hospitalization).

## Internet and mobile data

- Activate an eSIM before arriving in Paris and have data from minute one, without depending on the airport Wi-Fi or looking for a physical SIM.
- It is the most practical option for most nomads: it is activated from the mobile phone before even leaving home, without queues or paperwork upon arrival.

## Data sources

- Numbeo Cost of Living Index (without rent), calculated using New York as a reference and converted to euros at the current exchange rate.
- Average fixed broadband speed by country, Speedtest Global Index (Ookla).
- Numbeo Safety Index (residents' perception of safety), rescaled to a score out of 10.
- Numbeo Quality of Life Index (combines purchasing power, health, climate, cost, traffic and pollution), rescaled to a score out of 10.
