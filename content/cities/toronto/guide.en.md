## Visa and taxation

- Spanish citizens can enter Canada as visitors without needing a traditional visa, but they must apply for an electronic travel authorization (eTA) before flying, a quick and low-cost online procedure.
- The problem is that Canada does not currently have a digital nomad visa: the common practice of working remotely for a foreign employer or clients while visiting is de facto tolerated at the border, but is not explicitly regulated by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), which places it in a legal gray area that requires discretion and honesty if asked at immigration control.
- In tax matters, anyone who maintains their habitual residence in Spain (spending more than 183 days a year in Spanish territory or having the center of their economic interests there) will continue to be a Spanish tax resident and must declare their overall income to the Tax Agency, regardless of the time spent working from Toronto.

## Health and medical insurance

- The Ontario Public Health System (OHIP) is not available to temporary visitors or non-residents: it only covers those who meet permanent residency requirements in the province, so no foreign digital nomad can access it during a tourist stay.
- This makes purchasing private travel medical insurance a practically mandatory requirement, since emergency medical care in Canada without coverage can be extremely expensive.

## Neighborhoods and coworkings

- For those who work remotely, the most recommended neighborhoods in Toronto combine good transportation connections, a young atmosphere and a range of cafes and coworking spaces.
- The Annex, near the University of Toronto, offers slightly more affordable prices and great university life.
- Queen West and Liberty Village are popular areas for young professionals, with old industrial buildings converted into lofts, bars and studios.
- In terms of coworkings, WeWork stands out (several locations, from about 370 CAD per month for a shared desk), East Room in Riverside (from 245 CAD/month) and WorkHaus, with flexible month-to-month memberships.

## Internet and mobile data

- Activate an eSIM before arriving in Toronto and have data from minute one, without depending on the airport Wi-Fi or looking for a physical SIM.
- It is the most practical option for most nomads: it is activated from the mobile phone before even leaving home, without queues or paperwork upon arrival.
